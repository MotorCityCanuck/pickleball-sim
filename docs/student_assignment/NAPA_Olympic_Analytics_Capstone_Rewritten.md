**1. Business Scenario**

The North American Pickleball Association (NAPA) has launched a
multi-year Olympic Team Selection and High Performance Analytics
Initiative designed to identify elite men's doubles, women's doubles,
and mixed doubles teams capable of competing successfully in future
Olympic competition. NAPA leadership believes that traditional scouting
and tournament observation alone are insufficient for evaluating player
readiness, partnership chemistry, regional competitiveness, and
long-term Olympic potential.

To address this challenge, NAPA has invited six independent
graduate-level analytics consulting teams to design and execute advanced

NAPA. Each student team will operate as a professional consulting
organization responsible for delivering an executive-grade proposal and
recommendations to the simulated NAPA Olympic Selection Committee.

The initiative combines enterprise analytics engineering, predictive
modeling, longitudinal player evaluation, simulation science, and
AI-assisted software development practices. Student teams will work with
large-scale historical and monthly batch datasets provided in
partitioned Parquet formats while implementing modern medallion-style
data pipelines and analytics architectures and reproducible engineering
workflows.

**3. Understanding Pickleball and Olympic Competition**

Pickleball is a rapidly growing paddle sport combining elements of
tennis, badminton, and table tennis. The sport is played on a smaller
court using solid paddles and a perforated plastic ball. Competitive
doubles pickleball emphasizes positioning, communication, reaction
speed, tactical coordination, and sustained consistency.

This project focuses exclusively on doubles competition. Every match
consists of two teams of two players each. Teams may be men's doubles,
women's doubles, or mixed doubles. Unlike purely individual sports,
doubles pickleball introduces significant interaction effects because
partnership chemistry, strategic compatibility, communication patterns,
and play-style complementarity strongly influence outcomes.

Olympic team selection therefore cannot rely solely on individual
ratings. High-performing athletes may not necessarily form
high-performing partnerships. This creates a complex analytical
environment involving team synergy analysis, longitudinal progression
modeling, confidence estimation, and probabilistic tournament
forecasting.

**5. Enterprise Architecture and Engineering Expectations**

The data architecture for this initiative follows a Bronze / Silver /
Gold medallion approach commonly used in enterprise data engineering
organizations. Students are expected to design reproducible data
engineering pipelines that support historical persistence, future
monthly ingestion, feature engineering, predictive modeling, validation,
and executive reporting.

- Bronze Layer: Raw ingestion and preservation of source datasets

- Silver Layer: Cleansed, validated, and enriched analytical datasets

- Gold Layer: KPI-ready datasets, feature tables, dashboards, and model
  outputs

- Historical persistence and time-aware validation are required

- Batch-oriented processing workflows are necessary to support monthly
  batch processing

- Reproducibility and deterministic processing controls

- Governance, lineage, and observability considerations
