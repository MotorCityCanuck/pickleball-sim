**Request for Proposal (RFP)\
NAPA Olympic Analytics Platform Initiative**

*North American Pickleball Association (NAPA)\
Graduate Data Science and Analytics Engineering Consulting Competition*

**1. Purpose of the RFP**

The North American Pickleball Association (NAPA) is soliciting
competitive proposals from six independent analytics consulting
organizations to support the design and development of a next-generation
Olympic Analytics Platform. The proposed platform will support Olympic
team selection, player evaluation, tournament forecasting, athlete
development, executive reporting, and strategic coaching operations.

This Request for Proposal (RFP) is intended to simulate a realistic
enterprise analytics consulting engagement in which consulting
organizations compete to deliver the strongest combination of analytical
sophistication, engineering rigor, operational practicality,
explainability, and executive decision-support capabilities.

**2. Business Context**

Pickleball is rapidly evolving into a globally competitive sport with
increasing emphasis on international competition and Olympic
participation. NAPA leadership believes that traditional scouting and
observational approaches alone are insufficient for identifying
Olympic-caliber athletes and partnerships across North America.

NAPA therefore seeks a modern analytics platform capable of leveraging
longitudinal historical data, predictive modeling, simulation, and
executive dashboarding to support evidence-based Olympic planning and
athlete development.

**3. Engagement Objectives**

- Identify elite Olympic-caliber doubles partnerships

- Support evidence-based Olympic roster selection

- Forecast future athlete development and Olympic readiness

- Simulate Olympic qualification tournament outcomes

- Provide executive-grade dashboards and KPI frameworks

- Support explainable and defensible analytical decision-making

- Establish enterprise-grade analytics engineering workflows

**4. Scope of Work**

Consulting organizations are expected to propose, design, and
demonstrate a comprehensive Olympic analytics platform operating on a
large-scale synthetic pickleball ecosystem containing more than 250,000
simulated players and 10 million historical matches.

The platform should support longitudinal analytics, predictive modeling,
simulation, ranking systems, confidence-aware evaluation, and executive
decision support while operating within a local development environment.

**Required Proposal Deliverables**

- Executive summary and strategic recommendations

- Analytics platform architecture and operating model

- Bronze / Silver / Gold medallion data strategy

- Data engineering and ingestion workflows

- Feature engineering and KPI strategy

- Predictive modeling and tournament simulation methodology

- Executive dashboards and analytical scorecards

- Data quality, observability, and governance approach

- Validation and experimentation methodology

- AI-assisted development workflow approach

- Final Olympic qualification tournament recommendations

**5. Required Analytical Outcomes**

**Olympic Qualification Tournament Invitations**

Consulting teams must identify the top 16 men's doubles teams, top 16
women's doubles teams, and top 16 mixed doubles teams recommended for
invitation to a simulated Olympic qualification tournament.

**National Player Rankings**

Consulting teams must produce the top 25 nationally ranked players for
both the United States and Canada using analytically defensible and
explainable methodologies.

**High Performance Development Camp**

Consulting teams must identify high-potential emerging athletes
recommended for invitation to a NAPA High Performance Development Camp.

**Olympic Qualification Tournament Simulation**

Consulting teams must execute a probabilistic tournament simulation
capable of estimating advancement likelihoods, upset probabilities,
medal probabilities, and projected Olympic qualification outcomes. This
simulation must be used to select the members of the Olympic team for
each country.

**International Competition Simulation**

Following selection of the Olympic team members for Canada and the USA,
the consulting team must run a simulated tournament to predict the
outcome of elite level competition.

**Appendix A -- Round-Robin Qualifying Tournament Format**

The Olympic qualification tournament will use a round robin format. In a
round robin tournament, every invited team plays every other team in the
same division exactly once. Separate round robin tournaments should be
modeled for men's doubles, women's doubles, and mixed doubles.

Each division includes 16 invited teams. Because every team plays the
other 15 teams one time, each division contains:

- 16 teams

- 15 matches per team

- 120 total matches per division

- 3 divisions: Men's, Women's, Mixed Pairs

Each match is played as a best-of-three-games match. This means the
first team to win two games wins the match. A match may end in two games
if one team wins both of the first two games, or it may require a third
game if the teams split the first two games.

Each game is played to 11 points, but a team must win by at least 2
points. For example, 11--7 is a valid final score, but 11--10 is not. If
the score reaches 10--10, play continues until one team leads by 2
points, such as 12--10, 13--11, or 15--13.

For Monte Carlo simulation, teams should model each match
probabilistically. A baseline win probability may be estimated using
team strength, player ratings, partnership chemistry, confidence, recent
form, regional adjustment, and other engineered features. That
probability can be used to simulate game outcomes and then aggregate
those games into match outcomes.

A simple simulation approach is:

1.  Estimate the probability that Team A beats Team B in an individual
    game.

2.  Simulate Game 1 using that probability.

3.  Simulate Game 2 using the same or adjusted probability.

4.  Simulate Game 3 only if each team has won one game.

5.  Award the match win to the first team with two game wins.

6.  Repeat for every scheduled round robin match.

7.  Repeat the entire tournament many times, such as 1,000 to 10,000
    simulations.

Tournament standings should be based first on total match wins. If teams
are tied, suggested tiebreakers include:

1.  Head-to-head match result

2.  Game win percentage

3.  Point differential

4.  Points scored

5.  Strength of schedule or simulated advancement probability

The simulation output should include expected match wins, probability of
finishing first, probability of finishing in the top four, probability
of qualifying, average game win percentage, average point differential,
and upset probability by matchup. Teams should clearly document their
assumptions about how team strength is converted into game-level or
match-level win probabilities.
